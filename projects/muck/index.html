Project 07 · Public service message

Muck: The Monster We Made

A curious child meets a funny trash creature — then discovers that every discarded wrapper feeds a flood-sized problem the whole street must solve together.

Format1–2 minute public-service story
RoleWriting · Story design · Art direction
DeliverablesScript · Hand sketch · 9-shot board
ToolsShot planning · AI prompting

Make the invisible cause visible.

Executive summary

On a rainy street, Ayaan notices a wrapper moving near a drain. It joins mud, bottle caps, and plastic to form Muck — a small creature that grows each time someone litters. When heavy rain returns, the blocked drain floods the street. Ayaan realizes the monster is not the real problem: the neighborhood made it.

“Litter never truly goes away. Bin your waste. Keep drains clear. Protect your street.”

Narrative strategy

The creature begins funny and harmless, giving the audience an inviting visual hook. Escalation turns the same visual rule — litter makes Muck stronger — into danger. This simple cause-and-effect design makes the civic issue understandable without a lecture.

Shot progression

Wide establishing shots narrow into the creature reveal, grow through a comic two-shot, then widen again as the street floods. A low angle makes the enlarged Muck threatening; an over-the-shoulder realization shot pivots the story toward collective action.

Resolution

Ayaan leads the neighbors to bag the waste and clear the drain. Muck shrinks as responsibility grows, ending the film on a concrete, achievable action rather than abstract awareness.

Storyboard breakdown

Nine shots. One clear consequence.

Shot type, action, dialogue, sound, camera movement, transition, and production notes are carried together on one readable board.
