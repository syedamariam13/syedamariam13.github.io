Project 06 · Animated public service message

The Golden Crack

After an accident leaves Pip cracked and forgotten, the elderly teapot risks breaking again to help her family — and learns that repair can honor a scar instead of hiding it.

Format9-shot animated short / PSM
RoleScreenwriting · Visual direction
ThemeResilience · belonging · care
ToolsScene plan · lighting · storyboard

A scar reframed as strength.

Executive summary

Pip is an 87-year-old porcelain teapot who has spent a lifetime serving a family tea shop. A sudden fall leaves a crack across her body. Believing she is useless, she is stored in the attic — until the shop struggles and she chooses courage over fear to help once more.

“We don’t hide what’s broken. We honor it.”

Visual arc

The opening uses warm high-key morning light to create belonging. The fall introduces a high angle, hard light, and sudden camera shake. Cool low-key attic imagery isolates Pip; red-orange light marks her difficult return. Soft, warm light arrives with compassion, while gold backlight transforms the crack into the visual center of the resolution.

Character arc

Pip moves from usefulness to shame, from isolation to courageous service, and finally to self-acceptance. The grandmother’s kintsugi repair does not erase what happened — it makes Pip’s history visible and valued.

Public message

The final direct-address shot gives the campaign idea a face: “What makes us feel broken is often what makes us most worth keeping.” A dissolve carries personal healing into community renewal before the closing fade.

Visual direction

Warmth, fracture, renewal.

Character and shot development map emotion through composition, scale, texture, and light.
